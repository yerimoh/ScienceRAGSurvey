M1 Knowledge Construction and Indexing

Construction Retrieval Integration Verification

Construction is the offline step that builds the index. It runs each unit of a knowledge source through an encoder that turns the unit into a searchable key, a passage into a dense vector, a molecule into a structural fingerprint, a raw signal into an embedding. Because this key takes a different form on each substrate, the rest of this section builds the index one substrate at a time.

Passage indexing of textual sources

Over the textual substrate, a unit and a query are both text, so is one text encoder shared between them, embedding each passage into a dense vector matched by similarity or indexing it as sparse lexical terms matched by word overlap. Systems differ mainly in that encoder and the corpus they run it over. MEDRAG builds sparse and dense indexes over a mixed medical corpus of tens of millions of snippets, BioRAG trains a PubMedBERT-based domain embedding over 22 million life-science abstracts, and HiPerRAG parses 3.6 million articles and indexes them with a contrastively fine-tuned dense encoder. The same construction extends to institution-private corpora that never leave the premises. MITRA scrapes and runs OCR on CMS analysis notes into an on-premise dense index, and the multi-facility framework of Sulc et al. builds per-facility indexes over accelerator logbooks. DUNE-GPT, the RHIC preservation assistant, AccGPT, and a tokamak-log copilot index the internal documentation of their collaborations, keeping the stack local to preserve closed data. Because passages are chunked, the recurring failure is the loss of context that spans chunk boundaries. A passage index serves Question Answering and Literature Synthesis.

Graph construction from relational sources

Over the relational substrate, the unit of retrieval is a subgraph, so has two parts, embedding each node's text label so a query can locate an entry node while keeping the graph structure itself as the index to traverse from there. Construction therefore extracts entities and typed relations and reconciles them to shared identifiers. KG-RAG links query entities to nodes of the SPOKE biomedical graph, MedGraphRAG uses a large language model to build a three-tier graph over patient documents, literature, and the UMLS vocabulary, and CG-RAG builds a chunk-level citation graph that links passages by intra-document and citation edges. The graph keeps every grounded or synthesized claim traceable through typed edges, but its recurring failure is the flattening of edge weights and relation semantics when a retrieved subgraph is verbalized for the generator.

Structure indexing of structured-entity sources

Over the structured-entity substrate, an object carries no text to embed, so encodes it in its own form, a fingerprint, a descriptor, or a learned graph encoder for its structure, or a table of computed or measured values when retrieval is by property. Systems differ in the representation they choose and how they organize it. f-RAG slices molecules into a fragment vocabulary ranked by molecular property, and Rag2Mol builds a molecular library specific to each target protein pocket. MMPT-RAG indexes a large database of matched molecular pair transformations mined from chemical and patent corpora, and RADiAnce builds a contrastive latent space over known protein interfaces so that binders can be retrieved across domains. Because proximity in a structural representation need not mean proximity in the property of interest, the recurring failure is the gap between structural and property space. A structure index makes Molecular Design and Materials Discovery reachable, since a novel candidate is assembled from indexed structures.

Cross-modal encoding of perceptual sources

Over the perceptual substrate, no encoder exists before construction, so and the query encoder must be trained together into one shared space, over the signal and over text, so that an inner product can later match them. This makes construction the heaviest of the four substrates, since without that alignment there is no index at all. MMed-RAG trains domain-specific image-report retrievers for radiology, ophthalmology, and pathology, FactMM-RAG trains a report retriever on factual image-report pairs mined with RadGraph, LaB-RAG predicts categorical labels on top of frozen image embeddings to filter which report examples are retrieved, MADGEN aligns a tandem mass spectrum with molecular scaffolds by contrastive learning, and CardioRAG encodes a twelve-lead electrocardiogram with a variational autoencoder. Because the shared space separates signals only along the axes its training pairs distinguished, the recurring failure is that any signal detail never named in the paired text is invisible to retrieval.

The construction choice fixes what a system can later search and which objective it can pursue. The same molecule is reachable by its structure only if it was fingerprinted at construction, and remains reachable only as text if it was stored as a passage.
