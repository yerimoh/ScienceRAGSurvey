O Operational Objective

A system's operational objective is the task it must serve, and each such task is operationalized by a benchmark that fixes the query, corpus, and ground truth of Eq. (:guide). What output the task demands sets all three: some answers lie ready in the corpus as a stored label, others are proposals it does not contain that only an external verifier can judge. We order the seven task families of scientific RAG, from question answering to hypothesis generation, by this distance from the corpus, and Table:benchmarks lists each with its scale and substrate.

O1

Grounding

24 systems reaching this objective →

Answer a question whose gold already lies in the corpus, retrieve, cite, answer. (Question Answering.)

Question Answering

Question answering poses a question with a determinate answer and scores the returned answer against a gold label. Text-only benchmarks cover clinical and biomedical questions in MIRAGE, a suite of five medical QA sets, and PubMedQA, yes/no/maybe questions over abstracts, with LitQA2 requiring answers found only in paper bodies. Broader science is covered by MaScQA for materials-exam problems scored against official answer keys and SciQ for crowd-sourced multiple-choice questions across biology, chemistry, earth science, and physics. Retrieval over a Relational source appears in SciQA, which answers SPARQL queries over the Open Research Knowledge Graph against a gold answer set. Cross-modal question answering pairs a question with non-text evidence over the Perceptual substrate, as in the pathology-image benchmarks PathMMU and Path-VQA. Question answering is the most saturated task in scientific RAG, because its gold labels make scoring automatic, but that same fixed gold cannot separate genuine retrieval from parametric recall, and overlap between corpus and benchmark inflates the reported score.

O2

Synthesis

9 systems reaching this objective →

Integrate evidence no single source states, verify claims and synthesize the literature. (Claim Verification, Literature Synthesis.)

Claim Verification

Claim verification presents a claim and scores a verdict of supported, refuted, or unverifiable, reconciled across the retrieved evidence over the Textual substrate. SciFact judges claims against abstracts over a small curated corpus, SciFactOpen extends verification to open-domain retrieval over a pool of half a million documents, and ContraCrow scales it to whole-paper contradiction detection validated against expert judgment. These benchmarks certify correct verdicts on a closed claim set, but the sharp performance drop from SciFact to SciFact-Open shows that they do not measure generalization to novel claims outside it.

Literature Synthesis

Literature synthesis poses a question whose answer no single source states and scores the integration across many sources. Long-form aggregation is scored for multi-paper coverage and citation support in ScholarQABench, for abstractive review summarization in PubMedRS-200, and for paragraph summaries in the ideal-answer track of BioASQ, with SQuAI judging answers directly against the evidence they cite rather than a reference text. These benchmarks retrieve over the Textual substrate, and synthesis that follows citation links extends to the Relational substrate in Multi-XScience, which generates a related-work paragraph from a paper and the abstracts of its references. Synthesis benchmarks measure whether an answer is citation-supported and covers a reference answer, but coverage is scored against a fixed expert reference rather than the full relevant literature, so completeness is only partially certified.

O3

Discovery

13 systems reaching this objective →

Propose an output the corpus does not contain, judged by an external verifier. (Property Prediction, Molecular Design, Materials Discovery, Hypothesis Generation.)

Property Prediction

Property prediction specifies an entity and a target property and scores the prediction against a held-out measurement. Curated-database recovery over the Structured-entity substrate covers molecular property and toxicity in MoleculeNet and the Therapeutics Data Commons, and protein function against time-delayed annotations in CAFA. Measured-response recovery covers gene-perturbation response in GEARS and PerturBench. Link prediction over the Relational substrate scores held-out biomedical edges in OpenBioLink and OGB-biokg, and spectrum-to-molecule elucidation over the Perceptual substrate scores recovered structures in MassSpecGym. A held-out reference contains only what has already been measured, so a correct prediction outside it is scored as wrong, and recovery certifies fit rather than mechanism, since a strong fit can be interpolation.

Molecular Design

Molecular design sets a target such as a protein pocket or a property profile and scores a proposed molecule the corpus does not contain. PMO defines a set of property-optimization objectives under a capped oracle-call budget, and CrossDocked2020 provides docking pockets against which a generated ligand and its 3D pose are scored for structural accuracy. These benchmarks retrieve over the Structured-entity substrate for molecular structure and over the Perceptual substrate when a 3D pose conditions generation. Design benchmarks score binding or property proxies computed in silico, so a high score certifies the proxy rather than the wet-lab outcome it stands for.

Materials Discovery

Materials discovery sets a design target such as a stable crystal or a catalytic surface and scores it by re-running a physics simulator over the Structured-entity substrate of crystal and surface records. Matbench Discovery scores thermodynamic stability against DFT-relaxed ground truth, ADsorbML re-evaluates each adsorption structure with a DFT single point, MLIP Arena tests dynamical consistency beyond static references, and ScienceAgentBench executes generated code against task success criteria. A simulator confirms candidates far faster than laboratory screening, but it cannot judge the limits of its own functionals, kinetics, or synthesis feasibility, bounding what a passing score establishes.

Hypothesis Generation

Hypothesis generation provides a research context and scores a proposed idea, mechanism, or pathway that has no stored answer to match, recovering it against a held-out reference rather than a lexical gold. Rediscovery benchmarks withhold findings published after a model's knowledge cutoff and score how closely a generated hypothesis matches the withheld one, MOOSE-Chem scoring chemistry hypotheses against expert-annotated 2024 papers and ResearchBench decomposing discovery into inspiration retrieval, hypothesis composition, and ranking a gold hypothesis above generated negatives across twelve disciplines. HypoBench measures the recovery rate of ground-truth patterns on synthetic tasks, and TruthHypo scores the groundedness of biomedical hypotheses against a knowledge graph and a domain-specific corpus over the Relational substrate. These benchmarks retrieve inspirations over the Textual substrate of prior literature and, in TruthHypo, over a Relational knowledge graph. Because their verifier is a similarity match to a held-out finding or a groundedness check rather than a physical simulator, hypothesis generation sits at the weakest verification of the seven tasks, though contamination-controlled rediscovery makes its scores more reproducible than open-ended novelty rating.
