K Knowledge Source

The form in which a source stores its knowledge determines what can be retrieved from it. Text can be reached by text, but much of science is not text: a molecule, a graph of typed relations, or a raw instrument signal shares no words with a query, so none of them can be searched the way a passage is. Because a source's native form dictates whether a query can reach it at all, we organize knowledge sources by that form into four retrieval substrates, Textual, Relational, Structured-entity, and Perceptual, and describe what each one stores. Table:knowledge_source catalogs representative resources under each substrate.

Txt

Textual

22 systems on this substrate →

The textual substrate is the prose record of science, the papers, abstracts, clinical notes, and guideline documents in which findings are written down. It is the largest and most accessible of the scientific data types, and the only one already cast in the natural language a query is posed in.

General-purpose and domain literature

General-purpose corpora carry no disciplinary boundary, so a single pipeline serves many fields. Crossref is the registry of DOIs and their publication metadata, and ORCID the companion registry of author identities. OpenAlex aggregates these with PubMed and repository records into an open index of works, authors, venues, institutions, and concepts. S2ORC adds parsed full text in a unified citation-linked space, while Scopus, Web of Science, and Dimensions provide abstract-and-citation metadata and CORE aggregates open-access full text, under varying access terms. Domain indices serve one field with richer metadata. PubMed indexes biomedical abstracts under the MeSH vocabulary, Europe PMC adds open full text, and Embase covers the pharmacology literature commercially. NASA ADS covers astronomy, INSPIRE-HEP high-energy physics, and GeoRef the geosciences, while the IPCC assessment reports form a canonical climate-synthesis corpus. These are also repackaged into ready-made retrieval corpora: MedCorp combines PubMed, StatPearls, textbooks, and Wikipedia, MatSciKB does the same for materials science, and PMC Open Access supplies open full text.

Preprint literature

Preprints trade peer review for recency, offering the most current view of active science. arXiv is the multidisciplinary anchor over a public OAI-PMH interface and bulk S3 access, with author-assigned subject categories. Field servers complement it where arXiv's coverage is limited, including bioRxiv, medRxiv, ChemRxiv, EarthArXiv, and OSF Preprints.

Institution-private corpora

Institution-private corpora are collaboration wikis, logbooks, and internal notes that share the same textual substrate but stay behind access restrictions. On-premise systems retrieve over them while keeping the data private, as in MITRA over CMS collaboration documentation and DUNE-GPT over Fermilab's internal resources.

Rel

Relational

8 systems on this substrate →

The relational substrate is the curated web of scientific relations, the graphs that record how genes, diseases, drugs, and concepts connect. Its knowledge lives not in any single node but in the edges between them, the asserted links that no one document states in one place.

Curated concept-relation graphs

These are expert-curated graphs of asserted relations among canonical scientific entities. UMLS unifies biomedical vocabularies, while SNOMED CT and ICD-10/11 supply clinical and diagnostic terms. PrimeKG and DRKG assemble integrated drug, disease, and target relations, while STITCH records chemical-protein interactions. STRING protein-interaction data, KEGG, Reactome, and Gene Ontology map pathways and functional annotation, and the disease-gene resources OMIM, DisGeNET, and ClinVar link variants to phenotypes.

Literature-derived graphs

The same primary literature, once its citations and shared entities are linked, forms a graph rather than a set of passages. OpenCitations and the citation layers of OpenAlex and S2ORC expose reference edges between papers, over which task-specific graphs of papers and their extracted concepts are built.

Str

Structured-entity

7 systems on this substrate →

The structured-entity substrate is the objects of science themselves, the molecules, crystals, and materials held as data rather than described in words. Carrying no text, they can be reached only in their own terms, by matching a structure or a target property.

Molecule and structure libraries

These are libraries of molecular and 3D structure. The molecule libraries ChEMBL, ZINC, and PubChem hold compound records, and the 3D structure archives PDB, AlphaFold DB, and CrossDocked2020 hold atomic coordinates. A 2D fingerprint cannot capture 3D shape and a SMILES string drops stereochemistry, so the geometry that governs binding survives only in the 3D-coordinate form.

Property databases

These are property tables built from large-scale computation or measurement. Materials Project, OQMD, AFLOW, and Open Catalyst provide DFT-computed materials properties, QM9 and QMugs provide molecular properties, and BindingDB provides measured binding affinities.

Prc

Perceptual

9 systems on this substrate →

The perceptual substrate is the raw output of scientific instruments, the images, spectra, and signals recorded straight from measurement. They carry no words at all, so nothing can be retrieved from them until an encoder is learned to connect a signal to a text query.

Medical and biological imaging

Medical imaging dominates this modality. MIMIC-CXR and IU-Xray pair chest X-rays with radiology reports, and pathology and ophthalmology corpora extend the setting. The neuroimaging cohorts ADNI, OASIS, and ABIDE, together with the histopathology atlases TCGA (via the Cancer Imaging Archive) and the Human Protein Atlas, provide further imaging substrates.

Spectral libraries

Spectral libraries store the raw signal traces that identify a molecule. Mass-spectral libraries such as MassBank and GNPS, together with the spectral layers of HMDB, are the entry point for de novo structure elucidation from raw spectra.

Instrument arrays and time series

Arrays and time series are the numerical readings streamed from instruments, and no standard encoder for them exists yet. Sky-survey archives (SDSS, Gaia, MAST ), earth-observation grids (NASA EOSDIS, CMIP6, NOAA NCEI, USGS EROS ), physiological ECG traces, and the collision records of CERN Open Data are among the largest open archives in science, yet almost none are indexed as a retrieval corpus by any cataloged scientific RAG system.
