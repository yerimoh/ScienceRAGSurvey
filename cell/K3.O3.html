Section overview — § Weakly-verified Hypothesis Generation

Click any superscript chip in the paragraph to see verbatim evidence; the cited papers are listed below.

A final form of hypothesis is one in which the system generates a candidate that no strong external verifier can directly check, so that evaluation falls back to downstream task accuracy, expert validation, or recovery against a held-out reference rather than to docking, database lookup, or simulation. The output is typically a literature-derived hypothesis, such as a cellular-response prediction, a biomedical link prediction, or a molecular structure inferred from a non-textual signal, and the absence of an in-loop verifier means that novelty and plausibility carry more of the evaluation burden than physical correctness does. Evaluation in this setting consists of distributional similarity for predicted perturbation responses, held-out edge recovery for link-prediction tasks, and Top-K accuracy or Tanimoto similarity against held-out reference structures for measurement-derived candidates, instantiated across retrieval-augmented perturbation prediction 12345, biomedical link prediction 67, and MS/MS-driven molecular discovery 8. These metrics certify that the generated hypotheses recover known cases or align with expert intuition, but the absence of a strong external verifier leaves the K3.O3 and K4.O3 cells of the catalog largely empty, a sparsity we revisit in \S\ref{sec:frontiers} as a concrete frontier for future scientific RAG.

  1. PT-RAG — Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Predicting Cellular Responses to Gene Perturbation

    PT-RAG (Perturbation-aware Two-stage RAG): GenePT semantic retrieval + Gumbel-Softmax cell-type-aware selection. Outperforms STATE and vanilla RAG on Replogle-Nadig single-gene perturbation in distributional similarity W1, W2 (arXiv:2603.07233 §Abstract).

    Full summary →

  2. Replogle-Nadig — Mapping information-rich genotype-phenotype landscapes with genome-scale Perturb-seq

    Genome-scale Perturb-seq atlas — foundational single-cell perturbation dataset across thousands of essential genes; substrate for PT-RAG (Replogle et al., Cell 2022).

    Full summary →

  3. PerturBench — PerturBench: Benchmarking Machine Learning Models for Cellular Perturbation Analysis

    PerturBench: "comprehensive framework for modeling single cell transcriptomic responses to perturbations, aimed at standardizing benchmarking in this rapidly evolving field" with RMSE + rank metrics across four generalization regimes (arXiv:2408.10609 §Abstract).

    Full summary →

  4. GEARS — Predicting transcriptional outcomes of novel multigene perturbations with GEARS

    GEARS: GNN integrating gene-gene knowledge graph with perturbation embedding. Trained on Norman 2019 (102 single + 131 two-gene). Metrics: MSE on top-20 DEGs, Pearson correlation, Precision@10 for GI prediction (Nat. Biotechnol. 42:927-935, 2024).

    Full summary →

  5. Norman 2019 — Exploring genetic interaction manifolds constructed from rich single-cell phenotypes

    Norman et al. 2019 Science: 287 dual-CRISPRi gene-pair perturbations in K562 cells. Defined "GI manifold" via rich single-cell phenotypes — the canonical dual-gene held-out evaluation substrate (Science 365:786-793, 2019).

    Full summary →

  6. OpenBioLink — OpenBioLink: a benchmarking framework for large-scale biomedical link prediction

    OpenBioLink: "a large-scale, high-quality and highly challenging biomedical link prediction benchmark to transparently and reproducibly evaluate" embedding methods. Leakage-controlled held-out edge recovery (Bioinformatics 36:4097-4098, 2020).

    Full summary →

  7. OGB / OGB-biokg — Open Graph Benchmark: Datasets for Machine Learning on Graphs

    OGB suite (NeurIPS 2020) — includes ogbl-biokg for biomedical KG link prediction (drug-disease-protein edges) with hits@K public leaderboard. Same authors as GEARS (Leskovec lab).

    Full summary →

  8. MassSpecGym — MassSpecGym: A benchmark for the discovery and identification of molecules

    MassSpecGym: 231K MS/MS spectra of 29K molecules with leakage-controlled MCES splits. Three challenges: de novo molecular structure generation, molecule retrieval, spectrum simulation (NeurIPS 2024 / arXiv:2410.23326 §Intro).

    Full summary →
