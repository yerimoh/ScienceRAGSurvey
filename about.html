Scientific Retrieval-Augmented Generation: A Survey and Taxonomy

Oh et al., Vision and Learning Lab, Seoul National University

Abstract

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) grounds large language models in external knowledge, and scientific RAG has emerged as a distinct research frontier. Yet no survey treats it as a field in its own right. Existing reviews subsume it under general-purpose RAG and organize systems by retriever-generator pipelines, overlooking what sets scientific practice apart: what knowledge a system draws upon and what operation it performs on that knowledge. We present the first comprehensive survey of scientific RAG, using a 331-paper catalog to organize the field around the pipeline a system runs. The knowledge it retrieves is a substrate, namely Textual, Relational, Structured-entity, or Perceptual, whose native form fixes the matching operation it admits. The task it serves fixes the ground truth that scores its output, and rises by how far that truth sits from the corpus, from grounding through synthesis to discovery. The method connecting them specializes each stage, up to how deeply a verifier couples into generation. Across the catalog, a field's maturity tracks whether its knowledge can be made retrievable and its outputs verifiable. We close with a research agenda spanning adapting general RAG capabilities, building the components the field still lacks, and confronting the limits set by tacit and access-restricted knowledge.

How the survey organizes the field

A scientific RAG system is read through the pipeline it runs, along three axes. Each has its own section of this site, mirroring the survey.

K

Knowledge Source

The retrieval substrate, the native form a source stores its knowledge in, which fixes the matching operation it admits: Textual, Relational, Structured-entity, Perceptual.

 O

Operational Objective

The task a system serves, ordered by how far its ground truth sits from the corpus: from grounding a stored answer, through synthesis across sources, to discovery an external verifier must judge.

 M

Method — the pipeline

How evidence is carried to a claim, specialized at every stage — construction, retrieval, integration, verification — up to how deeply a verifier couples into generation.
