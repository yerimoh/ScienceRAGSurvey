#### 📌 TL;DR
The clinical characterization paper of the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI), which evaluates clinical progression indicators of neuroimaging and chemical biomarkers across normal aging, mild cognitive impairment, and Alzheimer's disease cohorts.

#### 🎯 Background
**Limitations of existing infrastructure**
- Lack of large-scale standardized data to longitudinally evaluate the clinical utility of neuroimaging measures and chemical biomarkers (CSF)
- Insufficient evidence data for the use of biomarkers in Alzheimer's disease clinical trials

**Why this system is needed**
- Need for integrated evaluation of neuroimaging and CSF biomarkers to predict MCI and AD progression
- Construction of a standardized multi-site cohort usable in clinical trial design

#### 🔨 Architecture
A North American multi-site study with a total of 819 subjects—normal cognition (229), mild cognitive impairment (MCI, 398), and mild Alzheimer's disease (AD, 192)—observed at baseline and 12-month follow-up. MRI, FDG-PET, CSF Aβ-42/tau measurements, and standard cognitive function tests such as ADAS-Cog were performed. The 12-month dementia conversion rate of MCI was 16.5%/year. CSF Aβ-42 significantly distinguished the three groups and predicted 12-month cognitive change.

#### ⚠️ Limitations
- As a baseline characterization paper, it does not include long-term longitudinal data
- About 50% of MCI subjects were taking anti-dementia drugs — possible confounding of biomarker interpretation
- Possible bias toward predominantly white, highly educated, and zip-code selection
