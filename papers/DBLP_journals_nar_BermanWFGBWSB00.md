#### 📌 TL;DR
The world's single, unique archive aggregating three-dimensional structural data of biological macromolecules (proteins, nucleic acids, etc.), describing the deposition, access, and future development plans for structures determined experimentally by X-ray crystallography, NMR, electron microscopy, and other methods.

#### 🎯 Background
**Limitations of existing infrastructure**
- No standard archive unifying protein structure data scattered around the world
- Lack of consistency in accessibility, format, and quality of structural data
- Inadequate data deposition and validation systems

**Why this system is needed**
- A single global archive is required for long-term preservation and access of structural biology data
- A standardized deposition system is required for experimental structures from X-ray crystallography, NMR, electron microscopy, etc.

#### 🔨 Architecture
Led by RCSB (Research Collaboratory for Structural Bioinformatics, Rutgers University). The world's single, unique archive aggregating experimentally determined three-dimensional structures of biological macromolecules (X-ray crystallography, NMR spectroscopy, electron microscopy, etc.). Equipped with a data deposition system, quality validation, and web-interface access. Future development plans discuss data standardization and functional expansion.

#### ⚠️ Limitations
- Deposition bias: concentration on structures of high research interest
- Crystallization conditions may differ from physiological environments
- Under-representation of dynamic behavior and intrinsically disordered protein regions
