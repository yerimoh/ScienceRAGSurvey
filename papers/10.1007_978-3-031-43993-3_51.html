PMC-OA / PMC-CLIP: Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training Using Biomedical Documents

MICCAI 2023 | Multimodal Dataset + Foundation Model | medical · bio Lin, Zhao, Zhang, Wu, Wu, Zhang, Wang, Xie — Shanghai Jiao Tong University / Shanghai AI Lab DBLP: conf/miccai/Lin23 · arXiv:2303.07240

TL;DR

1.65M biomedical image-text pairs collected through an automated pipeline from the 2.47M papers of the PubMed Central Open Access subset. Through fine-grained alignment at the subfigure-subcaption level (61.0%), this is a cleaned dataset 8× larger than the existing MedICaT (217K, 75% left as compound figures as-is). The accompanying PMC-CLIP model, when pretrained on PMC-OA, achieves R@10 +8.1%p over the SOTA on ROCO retrieval.

Background

Limitations of existing medical multimodal datasets (excerpt from paper §1)

"These existing biomedical image-caption datasets are still far from satisfaction for two reasons: First, the size of the existing datasets is limited (e.g., ROCO with 81K, MedICaT with 217K). Second, the existing datasets contain a large portion of compound figures (e.g., 75% in MedICaT) without separating into subfigures and aligning to corresponding subcaptions, resulting in noisy supervision signals."

Core problems:

  • Many figures in medical papers are compound figures (multiple subfigures within a single figure, e.g., (A) Macroscopic, (B) H&E section, (C) Microscopic)
  • Existing datasets match the entire caption to the entire compound figure → a great deal of noise during CLIP training
  • In contrast, PubMed Central holds 2.47M papers + 12.21M figure-caption pairs → a large leap is possible when cleaned with an automated pipeline

Construction Methodology — 6-step Pipeline

Step 1 — Collect figure-caption from PubMed Central
  └─ Time point: 2022-09-16
  └─ Covers 2,478,267 papers (2~20× vs ROCO 1,828,575 / MedICaT 131,410)
  └─ Extracted: 12,211,907 figure-caption pairs

Step 2 — Biomedical filtering (keyword + image classifier)
  └─ Using a keyword dictionary (based on UMLS T060 "Diagnostic Procedure"),
     remove figures whose caption has no medical keyword
  └─ ResNet-101 (trained on the 28 DocFigure classes): only figures whose
     "Medical" class falls within the Top-4 pass
  └─ Result: 381,096 medical figures

Step 3 — Compound figure separation (Conditional DETR detector)
  └─ ResNet-34 backbone, 4 encoder + 4 decoder layers, 32 learnable queries
  └─ Trained on 2,069 manually separated instances from MedICaT (= MedICaTSub)
  └─ Performance: mAP@0.5 = 0.94, precision = 0.93, recall = 0.94 (conf 0.7)
  └─ Result: 1,848,729 raw subfigures

Step 4 — Subfigure re-filtering (remove non-medical subfigures)
  └─ Re-apply the classifier from Step 2
  └─ Result: 1,646,592 subfigures from 378,717 compound figures
     (avg 4.3 subfigures / caption)
  └─ = PMC-OA-Beta (subfig + full caption matched only)

Step 5 — Subcaption separation (caption distributor [Yu Yu et al. 2017])
  └─ 239,905 separable captions → 898,467 subcaptions
  └─ Corresponding to 1,003,911 subfigures

Step 6 — Subfigure ↔ Subcaption alignment
  └─ Contrastive finetune a CLIP-style model pretrained on PMC-OA-Beta
     on MedICaTSub (alignment acc 73% on test)
  └─ Result: 1,003,911 fine-grained Subfig-SubCap pairs
     + 642,681 coarse Subfig-Cap pairs (cases that could not be separated)

Output (PMC-CLIP model architecture)

Model configuration:

  • Vision encoder: ResNet50 (from scratch)
  • Text encoder: PubmedBERT (initialized)
  • Fusion: 4-layer transformer
  • Loss: ITC (CLIP-style contrastive) + MLM with visual cues (λ=0.5)
  • Input resolution: 224 × 224

Loss function (excerpt from paper §3):

"Specifically, we denote batch size as b, we calculate the softmax-normalized cross-modality dot product similarity between the current visual/text embedding (v / t) and all samples within the batch, termed as p_{i2t}, p_{t2i} ∈ R^b, and the final ITC loss is: L_ITC = E_{(I,T) ∼ D} [ CE(y_{i2t}, p_{i2t}) + CE(y_{t2i}, p_{t2i}) ]"

Example cases (direct citation from paper Figure 2 — Step 5 Caption Distributor operation example)

🔬 Compound Figure separation example (excerpt of the Figure 2 visual sequence)

The pipeline diagram in the paper's Figure 2 shows the entire process by which an actual compound figure is processed in PMC-OA.

Original compound figure caption (example subcaptions separated in Step 5):

"(A) Macroscopic view of primary lung tumor tissue ......" "(B) Three representative H&E stained sections of FFPE lung tissue were scanned ......" "(C) Microscopic view at ×40 magnification ......"

→ The above caption was a single string in the original, but the caption distributor recognizes the (A)/(B)/(C) markers and separates it into 3 subcaptions. At the same time, the DETR detector detects 3 subfigure boxes from the same compound figure → the fine-tuned CLIP-style aligner matches (A) ↔ "Macroscopic view of primary lung tumor", (B) ↔ "H&E stained sections", (C) ↔ "Microscopic view at ×40".

🌳 Non-separable example (Step 5 fail)

"Figure 4. Forest Regeneration (A) Dense ...... (B) The almost ......"

→ Some captions are too short or structurally unclear for the distributor to separate (642,681 pairs, 39%, out of the total 1.65M). In this case, they are included in PMC-OA in the form of subfigure + full caption (coarse pair).

📊 Dataset statistics (excerpt from paper §2.2)

"Fig. 3(a), PMC-OA covers a wide range of diagnostic procedures, spanning from common (CT, MRI, X-ray) to rare ones (mitotic figure), which is much diverse than before."

"In PMC-OA, diseases are given in the free-form text, allowing for elaborate identification and analysis. For instance, eye diseases can be further categorized into Cataracts, Conjunctivitis, Macular degeneration, fish eye disease, etc."

"We provide the sex-ratio across ages in Fig. 3(d), as we can see PMC-OA is approximately gender-balanced, with 54% males."

🎯 Alignment accuracy (excerpt from Step 6)

"We split the subset into train and test by 3:1, and the finetuned model achieves alignment accuracy=73% on test set. We finally align 1,003,911 subfigure-subcaption pairs. Along with the remaining 642,681 subfigure-caption pairs, we termed this dataset as PMC-OA."

Limitations

  • Incompleteness of the caption distributor: only 239,905 separable captions were processed → the remaining 39% are kept as coarse subfig-cap pairs.
  • 73% alignment accuracy: subfig↔subcap matching is not perfect (relies on CLIP-style finetune).
  • Automated keyword/classifier-based filter: some non-medical figures may pass through / some medical figures may be missed.
  • Absence of author/institution metadata: figure source paper citation is possible, but patient metadata (age/disease ground truth) relies only on the caption text.
  • 2022-09-16 snapshot: subsequent new papers in PMC OA require a separate re-run of the pipeline (the paper states it is "continuously updatable").
  • Copyright concerns: although limited to the PubMed Central Open Access subset, some figures are under a CC BY-NC license — restricting commercial use.
