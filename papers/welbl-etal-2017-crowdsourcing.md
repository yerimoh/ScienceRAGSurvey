# SciQ: Crowdsourcing Multiple Choice Science Questions

> WNUT @ EMNLP 2017 (pp. 94–106) | Benchmark | bio · chem · physics · earth science
> Welbl, Liu, Gardner — UCL / University of Washington / Allen Institute for AI

## TL;DR
A set of **13,679** four-choice science questions covering the 4 fields of physics, chemistry, biology, and earth science, based on passages extracted from textbooks ranging from elementary/middle/high school to introductory college level, generated by crowdworkers **reviewing and revising AI-proposed distractors**. Two versions are provided (MC + direct-answer), with quality so high that humans cannot distinguish SciQ items from real exam questions. A retrieval-heavy dataset in which the RAG retriever baseline (Lucene) outperforms neural readers. Built on a total budget of **$10,415**.

## Background
- **The core bottleneck of science-exam QA**: the absence of large-scale in-domain training sets.
- In the 2016 Kaggle 8th-grade science exam challenge, even the best system achieved only **60% accuracy** (retrieval-based approaches outperformed neural readers).
- Difficulties of crowdsourcing: (1) lack of domain expertise, (2) difficulty ensuring question diversity and relevance, (3) **if distractor quality is low, the question becomes trivial**.
- The paper's core idea: reframe the human task from "generating from scratch" to "select / modify / validate on AI proposals" → fast, cheap, and diverse.

## Construction Methodology

```
Step 0 — Domain corpus collection
  └─ 28 textbooks (CK-12, OpenStax, and other Creative Commons learning materials)
  └─ Areas: biology · chemistry · earth science · physics
  └─ Level: elementary → college introductory

Step 1 — Automatic selection of relevant paragraphs with a noisy classifier
  └─ Only "in-domain" passages shown to workers → irrelevant passages removed

Step 2 — Crowdworker question generation (AMT Master's status, $0.30/HIT)
  └─ 3 passages presented at a time → worker picks 1 and writes a short-answer QA
  └─ In 12.1% of cases the worker used all 3 passages → rejecting all was also allowed
  └─ 175 workers participated in total

Step 3 — Distractor Generation model training
  └─ Random forest binary classifier:
      "Is this candidate a plausible distractor?"
  └─ Training data: false answers of real exam questions (positive)
                  + random expressions (negative)
  └─ Input features φ(q, a*, a'):
      WordNet similarity, distributional semantics, POS, phonetic, and many more

Step 4 — Crowd review and revision (AMT, $0.20/HIT)
  └─ Top distractor candidates proposed by the model are presented
  └─ Worker selects / revises / generates anew
  └─ Of the final distractors, 36.1% were generated directly by the model, about 50% accepted by workers

Step 5 — Quality validation
  └─ Distinguishability test: a mix of SciQ + real exam questions presented to separate workers
      → "cannot distinguish" → SciQ is of real-exam-level quality
  └─ Support passage attached to most items

Step 6 — Split and release
  ┌─────────────┬────────┬─────────────────────────┐
  │             │ MC ver │ Direct-answer ver       │
  ├─────────────┼────────┼─────────────────────────┤
  │ Train       │ 11,679 │ 10,481                  │
  │ Validation  │  1,000 │    887                  │
  │ Test        │  1,000 │    884                  │
  ├─────────────┼────────┼─────────────────────────┤
  │ Total       │ 13,679 │ 12,252                  │
  └─────────────┴────────┴─────────────────────────┘
  (reason direct-answer is smaller: some passages are withheld for copyright reasons)
  Release: AllenAI / huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/sciq
  Total budget: $10,415
```

## Data formats

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `question` | Science question text |
| `correct_answer` | Correct-answer choice |
| `distractor1` / `distractor2` / `distractor3` | The 3 incorrect-answer choices |
| `support` | Passage grounding the correct answer (included in most items; not used for reasoning in the MC version) |

## Real question examples (paper Fig. 1, first 4 of the training set, verbatim)

### Example 1 — Biology (Mesophiles)
> **Q:** *"What type of organism is commonly used in preparation of foods such as cheese and yogurt?"*
>
> 1) **mesophilic organisms** ← correct answer
> 2) protozoa
> 3) gymnosperms
> 4) viruses
>
> **Support:** *"Mesophiles grow best in moderate temperature, typically between 25°C and 40°C (77°F and 104°F). Mesophiles are often found living in or on the bodies of humans or other animals. The optimal growth temperature of many pathogenic mesophiles is 37°C (98°F), the normal human body temperature. Mesophilic organisms have important uses in food preparation, including cheese, yogurt, beer and wine."*

### Example 2 — Earth Science (Coriolis)
> **Q:** *"What phenomenon makes global winds blow northeast to southwest or the reverse in the northern hemisphere and northwest to southeast or the reverse in the southern hemisphere?"*
>
> 1) **coriolis effect** ← 정답
> 2) muon effect
> 3) centrifugal effect
> 4) tropical effect
>
> **Support:** *"Without Coriolis Effect the global winds would blow north to south or south to north. But Coriolis makes them blow northeast to southwest or the reverse in the Northern Hemisphere. The winds blow northwest to southeast or the reverse in the southern hemisphere."*

### Example 3 — Chemistry/Physics (Phase Change)
> **Q:** *"Changes from a less-ordered state to a more-ordered state (such as a liquid to a solid) are always what?"*
>
> 1) **exothermic** ← 정답
> 2) unbalanced
> 3) reactive
> 4) endothermic
>
> **Support:** *"Summary: Changes of state are examples of phase changes, or phase transitions. All phase changes are accompanied by changes in the energy of a system. Changes from a more-ordered state to a less-ordered state (such as a liquid to a gas) are endothermic. Changes from a less-ordered state to a more-ordered state (such as a liquid to a solid) are always exothermic."*

### Example 4 — Physics (Radioactive Decay)
> **Q:** *"What is the least dangerous radioactive decay?"*
>
> 1) **alpha decay** ← 정답
> 2) beta decay
> 3) gamma decay
> 4) zeta decay
>
> **Support:** *"All radioactive decay is dangerous to living things, but alpha decay is the least dangerous."*

### Example 5 (논문 p.2 본문 인용) — Force Reasoning
> *"With which force does the moon affect tidal movements of the oceans?"*
> → 자연 현상에 대한 추상적 이해 + 새로운 시나리오 적용을 요구 → 논문이 강조하는 "transfer of domain-specific background knowledge"의 대표 사례.

## 주요 평가 결과 (Table 2, MC version test set)

| Model | Accuracy |
|---|---|
| **Lucene** (IR retrieval, AllenAI textbooks) | **80.0** |
| Aristo (AllenAI과학시험 시스템) | 77.4 |
| AS Reader (neural) | 74.1 |
| GA Reader (neural) | 73.8 |
| TableILP (table-based) | 31.8 |
| **Humans** | **87.8 ± 0.045** |

**핵심 발견 #1**: Lucene IR baseline이 신경망 reader(AS/GA)을 **5–6pp 능가** — SciQ는 retrieval-heavy 데이터셋임을 입증. 실제 시험 환경에서도 retrieval-based가 우위.

**핵심 발견 #2**: TableILP는 hand-collected 백그라운드 표에 의존 → SciQ의 분야 다양성을 커버 못해 31.8%로 추락.

**핵심 발견 #3**: SciQ를 추가 학습 데이터로 활용 시 **실제 4th/8th-grade 과학 시험에서 +3.3% 정확도 향상** (AS/GA Reader) — 도메인 외 평가에서도 transfer 효과 입증.

### Direct-answer 버전 (BiDAF on passage + question)
- BiDAF: **EM 66.7% / F1 75.7%** — SQuAD 대비 EM 1.3% / F1 1.6% 낮음.
- 그러나 작은 학습 셋(10k)으로 SQuAD(87.5k)에 근접한 성능 도달.

## 한계점
- **난이도가 낮음**: 고등학교 ~ 학부 초반 수준 → 전문 연구 수준 QA 평가에는 부적합.
- **크라우드소싱 특성상 문항 품질 불균일**: 일부 단순 사실 회상, 일부 추론 필요.
- **재료과학·천문학·고급 수학 문항 거의 없음** — 28권 교과서가 covered 도메인 한정.
- **텍스트 기반만**: 이미지·수식·표 활용 문항 없음.
- **Distractor 품질의 휴리스틱 한계**: 36.1%가 모델 생성 → 일부 워커 수용 distractor의 plausibility는 random forest classifier의 feature에 좌우.
- **MC 버전에서 support passage 제외**: 시스템이 own background knowledge 사용해야 함 → 정통한 RAG 시스템과 fit하나 closed-book QA로는 어려움.
