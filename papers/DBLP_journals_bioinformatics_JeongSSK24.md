# Self-BioRAG: Retrieval + Self-Reflection for Medical Reasoning
> Bioinformatics (ISMB) 2024 | Method | medical · bio

## TL;DR
Self-BioRAG is a 7B (and 13B) medical reasoning framework that specializes Self-RAG for the biomedical domain: the model itself decides "when to retrieve" (adaptive retrieval), retrieves evidence from a biomedical corpus with MedCPT, and then self-critiques the relevance, support, and usefulness of the evidence via reflection tokens (RET/REL/SUP/USE) to pick the best evidence and answer.

## Architecture (Self-BioRAG Architecture)
Self-BioRAG consists of three core components.

**(1) When-to-retrieve decision (Adaptive Retrieval).** The generator model directly generates a retrieval token (RET) to decide whether retrieval is needed. If the normalized probability `p(retrieve=Yes) / (p(retrieve=Yes) + p(retrieve=No))` exceeds the threshold δ=0.2, it retrieves. This lets it skip retrieval for questions that can be answered from parametric knowledge alone, and perform retrieval only when necessary.

**(2) MedCPT Retriever + biomedical corpus.** The retriever is MedCPT, a biomedical-specific retriever contrastively trained on 255M query–article pairs from PubMed search logs. The indexing targets are four kinds of biomedical corpora (documents are split into 128-word chunks with 32-word overlap).

| Corpus | # Documents | # Chunks |
|---|---|---|
| PubMed | 36,533,377 | 69,743,442 |
| PMC Full-text | 1,060,173 | 46,294,271 |
| Clinical Practice Guidelines | 35,733 | 606,785 |
| Medical Textbooks | 18 | 133,875 |

After retrieving top-k (k=10) from each source, gathering a total of 4k pieces of evidence, a reranking module selects the final top-k evidence most relevant to the query.

**(3) Reflection / Critique tokens (4 types).** Special tokens generated by the model that control retrieval, evidence evaluation, and self-evaluation.

| Token | Input | Output values | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RET** (Retrieve) | instruction (+output) | yes / no / continue | Is retrieval needed |
| **REL** (IsRel) | instruction, evidence | relevant / irrelevant | Does the evidence contain useful information |
| **SUP** (IsSup) | instruction, evidence, output | fully / partially / no support | Is the answer statement supported by the evidence |
| **USE** (IsUse) | instruction, output | 5 / 4 / 3 / 2 / 1 | How useful is the output for the question |

**(4) Generator.** A 7B model (and a 13B version) fine-tuned from Self-RAG weights based on a LLaMA2 backbone as the initialization. They report that starting from Self-RAG weights yielded better performance than training LLaMA2 directly.

## Pipeline (inference)
1. **Input:** receive an instruction.
2. **Retrieval-need decision:** the model generates a RET token and decides whether to retrieve based on the δ=0.2 criterion.
3. **Conditional retrieval:** when needed, MedCPT retrieves top-10 (4k total) pieces of evidence from each of the four corpora and reranking selects the final top-k.
4. **Per-passage critique / self-reflection:** for each evidence candidate, evaluate REL (relevance), SUP (support), and USE (usefulness) tokens.
5. **Evidence selection:** select the evidence with the highest critique score, a weighted sum of the normalized probabilities of REL, SUP, and USE (the weights are adjustable at inference time).
6. **Answer generation:** generate the answer by combining the selected evidence with parametric knowledge.

## Training
**(1) Critic model training (generating reflection data).** For 5,000 samples out of about 120k biomedical instructions, reflective tokens are annotated with the GPT-4 API to build the training data (epoch 3, lr 2e-5). This critic is then used to automatically attach reflection tokens to all instructions.

**(2) Generator model training (instruction tuning).** The generator is trained on 84,728 instructions filtered from those labeled by the critic. It is trained to generate the answer and reflection tokens together (epoch 5, lr 2e-5, A100 80GB×8, DeepSpeed stage 3, FlashAttention).

**(3) Instruction data.** Combines Mol-Instructions, MedInstruct, and self-generated biomedical instructions. The original 122,349 are filtered down to a final 84,728 used.

## Key results
**Multiple-Choice QA — Accuracy(%)**

| Model | Params | MedQA | MedMCQA | MMLU-Med | Avg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LLaMA2 | 7B | 35.2 | 36.3 | 46.3 | 39.3 |
| RAG | 7B | 36.2 | 38.3 | 47.7 | 40.7 |
| Self-RAG | 7B | 31.2 | 36.5 | 45.7 | 37.8 |
| **Self-BioRAG** | **7B** | **43.6** | **42.1** | **53.9** | **46.5** |
| Self-BioRAG | 13B | 48.6 | 44.0 | 57.2 | 49.9 |

An absolute improvement of about 7.2%p on average over the 7B open models.

**Long-Form QA — ROUGE (R1/R2/RL)**

| Model | LiveQA | MedicationQA |
|---|---|---|
| LLaMA2 | 8.8 / 1.9 / 6.2 | 5.7 / 1.2 / 4.4 |
| RAG | 11.5 / 2.3 / 11.1 | 9.8 / 1.3 / 4.8 |
| **Self-BioRAG** | **19.7 / 3.1 / 13.4** | **17.6 / 3.3 / 13.5** |

**Ablation (impact on average accuracy).** Removing reflection tokens −1.3%p, removing the biomedical corpus −2.9%p, removing the MedCPT retriever −4.4%p, removing biomedical instructions −7.3%p (the largest impact).

**Adaptive vs. fixed strategies (multiple-choice average).** No-retrieval 44.8 / Always-retrieve 46.2 / **Adaptive 46.5**.

## Limitations
- The retrieval corpus is limited to English PubMed/PMC/guidelines/textbooks, so domain and language generalization is limited.
- Critic training and evaluation take GPT-4 annotations as ground truth, so GPT-4's biases and errors can propagate into reflection quality.
- On multiple-choice, the 7B model still falls short of large closed models such as GPT-4, and the retrieval gains are not uniform across question types.
- ROUGE-based long-form evaluation does not directly measure factuality or clinical accuracy.
